Key insight

New users weren't leaving because of one big flaw. They left over small moments: a missing back button, a fiddly slider, a photo the app refused.

And some of what looked like technical problems were really about trust.

Project overview

BeTickled, a US startup, had launched a dating and social app on Android. New users were signing up and then disappearing, and the team didn't know where or why.

My role: UX researcher. I reviewed the recordings, rated every issue and wrote the recommendations.

1Observe: What do new users actually do?

Research question

Where do new users struggle or give up, and what is causing it?

Methods

Session recording analysis, heatmaps

Who and what

Around 450 recorded sessions from November 2020, in Smartlook

Recordings show what real users do without a researcher in the room, which was the fastest way to see problems across the whole app at once. I went module by module, from sign-up and login to the map, profiles, chat, settings and premium features.

Research impact

  • A full picture of the app: problems mapped across nine parts of the app, from sign-up to premium features, each with what users did and why it mattered.

2Rate: Which problems matter most?

Research question

Which problems should the team fix first?

Methods

Severity rating on a 1 to 5 scale

Who and what

21 issues across nine parts of the app

A missing back button restarted the whole sign-up

On the create-password screen there was no back button, and Android's own back button closed the app, so users had to start again. Severity 5.

Small controls caused big drop-off

Users dragged the age slider three or four times to set their age. They couldn't crop or rotate photos, so they left the app to edit and had to restart the upload. Severity 5.

New users didn't trust the app yet

Some users uploaded cartoons, animals or model photos to hide their identity. The app rejected them, and they left. Severity 4.

Familiar patterns were missing

Most users didn't know what the tick and cross buttons did. Search filters gave no feedback, and sometimes didn't apply. Severity 4 to 5.

Empty screens sent people away

About ten users opened chat, found no matches, and left. Severity 4.

Research impact

  • A clear fix order: 13 of the 21 issues were rated at the highest severity, so the team knew exactly where to start.

Reflection

What I learned

Recordings are excellent at showing what people do, but they only let you guess why. The photo finding was the clearest example: what looked like a technical rule was really about trust.

What I'd do differently

Pair the recordings with a few short interviews, so I could confirm the reasons behind the behaviour before recommending fixes.
