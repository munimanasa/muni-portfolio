30 interviews inside the library uncovered safety, access and wayfinding problems, presented to the library director.

Project overview

The David Wilson Library at the University of Leicester is open 24 hours. It has 1,500 study spaces, 13 group study rooms, 350 computer places and around 38 km of shelving. The library team launched a "kindness audit" to understand what people experience in the building and what they need.

Research question: What makes the library hard to use, and which problems matter most to fix?

1Plan: Who should we talk to?

Research question

Which of the library's users should we hear from first, and how do we reach them quickly?

Methods

Sampling plan from entry data, snowball recruitment

Who and what

Students and staff, the library's largest groups

The library serves students, teaching and non-teaching staff, library staff, locals and visitors. Entry data showed students and staff were by far the largest groups, so we focused on them. Our team of three came from three different departments, and each of us recruited in our own, with classmates referring friends.

Research impact

  • Fast, focused recruitment: three departments and snowball sampling let us reach 30 people quickly.

2Interview: What do people experience here?

Research question

What makes the library hard to use, in people's own words?

Methods

In-depth interviews inside the library, on-site observation

Who and what

30 interviews of about 30 minutes, with consent and recordings

  • 30interviews inside the library
  • 3researchers, 3 departments
  • 5priority problem areas
  • 24/7library, with no café after 10 PM

Holding interviews in the building mattered: several students walked us to the problem and showed us, which surfaced issues people would never think to mention in a meeting room.

Research impact

  • Problems no survey would find: on-site interviews showed us the exact places where the building let people down.

3Prioritize: Which problems matter most?

Research question

Which problems are most significant, and how do they group together?

Methods

Card sorting of every problem and need

Who and what

Every recording, listened back in full

Drinking water was hard to find, and hard to drink

There were no signs for drinking water. The only taps were inside the toilets, and some weren't labelled. The water was very cold, there were no kettles, and the café closed at 10 PM in a library open all night. I saw someone heating water with a room heater.

Useful services were invisible

The library lends laptops, but most people we interviewed didn't know. Headphones, mice and other equipment were at the reception desk, with no sign saying so.

The movable shelves had a hidden safety risk

Each unit has one handle to move it and one to lock it. If someone walks in without locking, another person can move the shelves from the other side while they're inside.

Wheelchair users met barriers all over the building

The laptop checkout reader and catalogue computers were above wheelchair reach, the lifts had no Braille, the accessible toilet needed a key users didn't have, and the accessible entrance door had been broken for a month.

People got lost, and booking a room was slow

Floor maps left out some rooms, the colour zones didn't say which subjects they held, and booking a room or computer couldn't be done inside the library.

Research impact

  • Five clear priorities: card sorting turned dozens of individual problems into five areas the library could act on.

4Present: What should change?

Research impact

  • Heard by decision makers: the library director and team welcomed the findings and planned to act on them.

Outcome

  • Five problem areasFrom drinking water to wheelchair access, each with a clear recommendation.
  • Presented to the directorThe library director and team welcomed the work and planned improvements.

Reflection

What I learned

This was my first time doing research in the wild, on a physical space. Being in the building with people showed me problems no survey would have found. It also showed me that the people a service most easily overlooks are often the ones who struggle most.

What I'd do differently

Recruit beyond our own classmates, so we also hear from locals, visitors and wheelchair users directly, and follow up after the presentation to see which changes were made.
